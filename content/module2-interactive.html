Module 2 learning plan

Module objectives

  • Calculate dose-to-volume, infusion rates, weight-based doses, and gravity drip rates.
  • Enter and interpret editable drug, concentration, weight, and pump-rate parameters.
  • Recognise start, pause, completion, and occlusion states in a syringe-driver simulation.

How to use the activities

Attempt each calculation before revealing the worked solution. Change one input at a time, check the units, and compare the result with your independent calculation. The simulator is educational only; it does not control a real device.

2.6 Additional Practice Scenarios

Scenario 1: Morphine for Trauma Pain

Patient: 32-year-old male, motorcycle accident, femur fracture. Weight 78 kg. Pain score 9/10.

Order: Morphine 0.1 mg/kg IV bolus.

Available: Morphine 10 mg/mL ampoules.

Calculate:

  1. Total dose required (mg)
  2. Volume to draw up (mL)
  3. Administration time (over 2-3 minutes)
Show Solution

1. Dose: 0.1 mg/kg × 78 kg = 7.8 mg (round to 8 mg for practical administration)

2. Volume: 8 mg ÷ 10 mg/mL = 0.8 mL

3. Administration: Give 0.8 mL slowly over 2-3 minutes IV push. Monitor for respiratory depression (RR <10), hypotension, and sedation. Have naloxone 0.4 mg ready as reversal agent.

Scenario 2: Noradrenaline Infusion for Septic Shock

Patient: 55-year-old female, septic shock, BP 75/40 despite 2L fluid resuscitation. Weight 62 kg.

Order: Noradrenaline 0.1 mcg/kg/min IV infusion.

Preparation: 4 mg noradrenaline in 250 mL 5% dextrose.

Calculate: Infusion rate in mL/hour.

Show Solution

Step 1: Dose per minute = 0.1 mcg/kg/min × 62 kg = 6.2 mcg/min

Step 2: Dose per hour = 6.2 mcg/min × 60 = 372 mcg/hour = 0.372 mg/hour

Step 3: Concentration = 4 mg ÷ 250 mL = 0.016 mg/mL

Step 4: Infusion rate = 0.372 mg/hour ÷ 0.016 mg/mL = 23.25 mL/hour (round to 23 mL/hour)

Monitoring: Titrate to MAP >65 mmHg. Check BP every 5 minutes. Watch for arrhythmias, extravasation (causes tissue necrosis). Use central line if available.

Scenario 3: Paediatric Midazolam for Status Epilepticus

Patient: 4-year-old child, continuous seizures for 8 minutes. Weight 18 kg.

Order: Midazolam 0.15 mg/kg IV.

Available: Midazolam 5 mg/mL ampoules.

Calculate: Dose and volume to administer.

Show Solution

Dose: 0.15 mg/kg × 18 kg = 2.7 mg

Volume: 2.7 mg ÷ 5 mg/mL = 0.54 mL (round to 0.5 mL)

Administration: Give 0.5 mL slowly over 2-3 minutes IV. Monitor respiratory rate, SpO₂, and BP. Prepare bag-valve-mask and suction. Have flumazenil available but use cautiously (may precipitate seizures if child is on chronic benzodiazepines).

Alternative route: If no IV access: 10 mg IM or 10 mg intranasal (5 mg per nostril using MAD device).

Scenario 4: Adrenaline 1:1000 to 1:10,000 Dilution

Patient: 68-year-old in cardiac arrest (VF/pVT), 3rd shock delivered, still in VF.

Order: Adrenaline 1 mg IV (1:10,000 concentration).

Available: 1:1000 adrenaline (1 mg/mL) ampoules only.

Task: Prepare the correct dilution.

Show Solution

Step 1: Draw up 1 mL of 1:1000 adrenaline (= 1 mg).

Step 2: Add 9 mL normal saline to the syringe (total 10 mL).

Step 3: This gives you 1 mg in 10 mL = 0.1 mg/mL = 1:10,000 concentration.

Step 4: Administer the entire 10 mL IV push during CPR.

Repeat: Every 3-5 minutes during ongoing CPR if still in VF/pVT.

Warning: Never give 1:1000 adrenaline IV without dilution—this is a 10-fold overdose and causes severe hypertension, arrhythmias, and myocardial ischemia.

Scenario 5: Fluid Bolus for Hypovolaemic Shock

Patient: 25-year-old male, penetrating chest trauma, BP 80/50, HR 130, cool peripheries. Weight 85 kg.

Order: 20 mL/kg fluid bolus (normal saline) stat.

Calculate: Total volume and administration strategy.

Show Solution

Volume: 20 mL/kg × 85 kg = 1700 mL

Administration: Administer rapidly over 10-15 minutes using:

  • Two large-bore IV cannulas (14G or 16G)
  • Pressure bag or manual push with 3-way tap
  • Blood tubing if available (wider bore)

Reassessment: After bolus, recheck BP, HR, capillary refill, lactate. If still shocked, repeat 20 mL/kg bolus and prepare for blood transfusion (activate massive transfusion protocol if available).

Caution: Avoid excessive crystalloid in penetrating trauma with uncontrolled bleeding ("permissive hypotension" strategy: target SBP 80-90 mmHg until haemostasis achieved).
